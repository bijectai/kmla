### 1. P-WIRE replacement wording

The two sentences are faithful to A-008's substance. Six defects, in descending
order of how much trouble each could cause.

1. **"fixed once in `Interface/`" cannot mean "implemented once".** The Lean
   oracle and the Python-side serializer cannot share an implementation across the
   language boundary. The sentence can only mean the byte-level rules are
   *specified* once in `Interface/` and mirrored. As written it invites a reader
   to assume a single implementation exists and therefore that divergence is
   impossible, which is precisely the failure the clause is meant to prevent.
2. **The enumeration omits `G1` and `A3`.** H6.1 defines a scalar entry point as
   "the *first* solution under **G1** order", so result semantics for every scalar
   target depends on G1, which the list does not name. And the input codec must
   preserve `Pat.wild` ids for the original cases, whose "identical only to
   itself" semantics is **A3**, also unnamed. Contract enumerations are read as
   exhaustive; a field governed by an unlisted section falls under the *second*
   sentence and is blocked.
3. **H6.3/H6.4 are ambiguous in scope.** They are not in the enumeration, yet the
   separate owner choices invoke H6.3 for original-case success. Either they
   belong in the list or the list should say it covers the wire form only and not
   per-case accuracy.
4. **"Producers remain blocked on any field those sections do not determine" is
   self-judging.** The producer decides whether a field is determined, with no
   recorded test. One option available to Dev, offered as a repair of the defect
   and not as a recommendation: require the codec's specification site to
   enumerate each wire field against the section that determines it, so an
   undetermined field is visible rather than implicit.
5. **"Approved canonicalization" and "builder-owned codec" are adjacent terms and
   the retained sentence can be read as forbidding the new one.** The intended
   line is that canonicalization fixes *which value is observed* (H6.2's sorted
   deduplicated set, ISO days, `Int` dollars) while the codec fixes *how bytes
   are written*. Nothing in the three sentences says that.
6. **The field-level framing does not authorize the record-level structure.**
   Neither replacement sentence mentions record granularity, so the separate owner
   choice is load-bearing: the two must be installed together or the granularity
   has no basis in PARITY.md.

### 2. The separate owner choices

**Granularity — one record per (Household, target, declared mode,
bound-argument tuple including year).** Three defects:

- **"Declared mode" has no declaration site yet.** It resolves to
  `Interface/S{N}.lean`, which PLAN Phase 2.3 has not produced. Until it exists,
  the record key names an artifact that does not.
- **The key collides with the ID grammar.** PARITY.md requires ids to match
  `[A-Za-z0-9][A-Za-z0-9_-]*`, and P-PARITY5 policy 4 adds uniqueness under ASCII
  case folding. A bound-argument tuple can contain `str "private home"` — spaces,
  and in general any `Term` string. A naive concatenation cannot be an id. If a
  hash is used, the id → (target, mode, arguments) mapping has to be recorded
  somewhere or every mismatch becomes undiagnosable.
- **It inflates the Checkpoint 2 gates.** One household yields many records, so
  "≥ 10k inputs" and "≥ 20 hits per arm" become much cheaper to satisfy in
  households than the plan's wording implies. Whether the gate should additionally
  count distinct households is Dev's call; the hazard is that it is satisfied
  without anyone deciding to relax it.

**Original-case identity retained independently, success still H6.3.** One item:
the case → record(s) mapping may be many-to-one and should be stated. The two
extra-conjunct cases `s152_d_2_D_pos/neg` are the concrete test, since H6.3
evaluates their conjuncts on each observed tuple.

**Encoding choices.** These are internally consistent and match what a standard
emitter produces: short `\b \f \n \r \t`, lower-case `\u00xx` otherwise, raw
non-ASCII, compact separators, decimal integers. That is exactly `json.dumps`
with `separators=(',', ':')` and `ensure_ascii=False`, which is what makes the
Python mirror cheap. Two observations, neither a defect: the sort inside
`observe` is unaffected because UTF-8 byte order and code-point order coincide,
so a Lean `Char`-wise sort and a Python code-point sort agree on byte-identical
encodings; and `/` and U+007F stay unescaped on both sides, which is consistent
with the current encoder. One genuine gap: "no normalization" should be stated to
cover astral-plane code points explicitly (raw, never surrogate-pair escapes),
since that is the one place a mirror implementation could silently differ.

### 3. Findings that bear on all three R5 routes

I verified these against the source; they are inputs to the audit rather than
opinions about it.

- **The group is closed.** `section152.pl` contains no call to §1, §2, §63, §68,
  §3301 or §3306, and `section7703.pl` calls exactly one predicate outside
  itself: `s152_a_1` at line 139. So §151, §2, §68 and §3306 call *into* the group
  and never receive a call back. **No cross-group cycle with R6's or R7's fuel is
  possible**, and a group-local counter cannot interact with theirs. This
  disposes of the joint-measure worry that would otherwise apply to all three
  routes.
- **Every cycle crosses `c1E`.** From `s7703` the only exit into §152 is
  `s7703_b_1 → s152_a_1 → s152_c → s152_c_1`, whose branches are c1A (utils
  relationship predicates), c1B (facts only), c1C → c3 → `is_descendent_of`, and
  c1E → `s7703`. `s7703_b_2` and `s7703_b_3` touch facts only
  (`section7703.pl:142-201`). Nothing in that subtree calls `s152/3` or
  `s152_d`. So the macro-decrement site is **sound as a cycle cut** for A, B and
  C alike. That is the strongest thing I can say in the menu's favour, and it is
  route-independent.
- **The menu's entry list is incomplete.** Q-011 names top-level c1E, source
  modes, R8 and stipulations. There are three further entries into `s7703` that
  the accounting must cover: `s152_b_2` (`section152.pl:45`), reached from
  `s152/3:7`; `gross_income/3` (`utils.pl:273-299`), reached from `s152_d_1_B`
  (`section152.pl:268`) and from §1/§63/§151; and §151's four direct calls
  (`section151.pl:7,21,71,168`). None of them lies on a cycle, by the closure
  argument above, so none breaks soundness — but each starts or inherits the
  counter and so belongs in the wrapper accounting before `N+1` is claimed.
- **Counter discipline is ambiguous and matters.** "R8 shares rather than resets
  it" can mean a depth counter copied down each branch, or a single budget
  consumed across sibling calls. Only the first is correct. Under the second, an
  evaluation that legitimately crosses c1E more than `N` times *in sequence* —
  §151 querying many persons, for instance — exhausts the budget with no
  recursion at all, and R9's "exhaustion is unreachable on `Valid` inputs" becomes
  false for a reason unrelated to cycles. Whichever route Dev picks, this needs
  one unambiguous sentence.
- **Placement interacts with `ValidStip`.** The originals are compared under
  `ValidStip` = V2–V9, which excludes V1. A rule landing in V1 therefore
  constrains generated inputs only and leaves the 376 unprotected; a rule landing
  in a new V-number constrains both and may drop originals from the Week-1 gate
  and from H4.1. This bites route A hardest, because A has two halves that want
  different homes — see below.

### 4. Route A — birth-function narrowing

**The acyclicity argument is valid as given**, and I checked each leg. Born →
born strictly increases a now-well-defined `dob`; a born → birthless step is
impossible to reverse, because alternative (i) needs the taxpayer's birth day and
the birthless person has none, forcing every later step onto (ii);
birthless → birthless descends the V4-acyclic child graph; self-edges fail both
alternatives. The transition relation is contained in K ∧ c3 because `s152_c_1`
evaluates A, B, C before E (`section152.pl:63-67`), so the restriction is an
over-approximation of the real edges, which is the safe direction. Under
acyclicity a branch visits distinct persons, so ≤ N−1 transitions and `N+1`
bounds the depth.

**Defects and obligations specific to A:**

- **A is not fact-list decidable, contrary to the impression V10 left.** Its
  antecedent is "K(T,D) holds and `s152_c_3(D,T,y)` succeeds", which needs the
  oracle's `s152_c_2` and `s152_c_3`. Only the uniqueness half is decidable from
  facts alone. A therefore imports `Oracle/` into `Valid` exactly as V7 and V8 do
  — permitted by H5, but it should not be sold as cheaper than they are.
- **The two halves want different homes.** Uniqueness ("at most one distinct
  birth event per person") is a V1-style well-formedness condition; the age/K
  condition is a V10-style exclusion. If uniqueness lands in V1 only, it does not
  apply to the originals under `ValidStip`, and an original containing a
  multibirth sibling configuration would still diverge on the Week-1 parity run.
  Dev has to place both halves deliberately.
- **"Distinct" needs a definition.** Under G4, `atom "e1"` and `str "e1"` are
  different terms. Uniqueness must say distinctness is tag-sensitive, matching the
  treatment Q-011 already specifies for U.
- **Stipulation coverage works by exclusion, not by proof.** A case that
  stipulates `s152_c_3` edges without birth evidence satisfies neither (i) nor
  (ii) and is excluded. That is coherent, but it means A's "covers c3
  stipulations too" is a claim about the domain shrinking, and the audit must
  report which originals drop out, by id.
- **One thing A does not cost that might be assumed:** an edge whose dependent has
  a birth event while the taxpayer has none is already impossible in the source
  (disjunct 1 needs both, disjunct 2 needs the dependent birthless), so A's rule
  excludes nothing extra there.

### 5. Route B — formal all-solution graph

**B is sound almost by construction**: it requires acyclicity of a relation that
contains the true transition relation, so the depth bound follows immediately and
`E_y` is strictly tighter than A's K ∧ c3 (it adds c1B's residence and date
conditions), hence excludes the fewest households of the three.

**Defects and obligations specific to B:**

- **The prefix must exclude c1E, and the installed text must say so.** `E_y` uses
  c1A ∧ c1B ∧ c1C. If c1E were included, `Valid` would depend on the very
  recursion it bounds. Q's phrasing gets this right; it is the load-bearing
  detail and is easy to lose in an edit.
- **"Rank = longest `E_y` path" cannot be the fuel.** Computing a longest path
  presupposes acyclicity, which is the `Valid` premise and is not available when
  the oracle's *definition* is elaborated. The rank belongs in the proof; the
  definition still wants a constant such as `N+1`, with rank ≤ N−1 as the lemma.
  As phrased, B asks the definition to depend on its own precondition.
- **Decidability is real but non-trivial.** `E_y` is computable by finite
  enumeration over U × U because c1A, c1B and c1C terminate — c1A and c1C reach
  only `utils` relationship predicates, with `is_descendent_of` bounded by R1
  under V4, and c1B reads facts. That argument needs writing down, and the
  `Decidable` instance needs building, before B's `Valid` can be used as a
  premise.
- **B's cost is schedule, not corpus.** Choosing B authorizes proof work whose
  size is not yet estimated, and Q-011 is right that it narrows `Valid` too: no
  proof can make the two witnessed cycles terminate.

### 6. Route C — coarse structural exclusion

**C is sound**: K ⊇ E_y ⊇ transitions, so K acyclic bounds the same recursion, and
K is the cheapest of the three to decide, needing only the relationship
predicates with no dates, ages, residences or marriages.

**One defect dominates everything else about C, and it is larger than the menu
says.** `is_sibling_of` (`utils.pl:131-156`) puts X and Y each in either the
`agent_` or the `patient_` position of the same `brother_`/`sister_` event, so for
any such event linking two distinct persons both `s152_c_2(b,a,_,_)` and
`s152_c_2(a,b,_,_)` have solutions via `s152_c_2_B` with `S65 == Bsss`. K
therefore contains a 2-cycle for **every sibling pair whatsoever**, and
`is_stepsibling_of` (`utils.pl:167-187`) is symmetric in the same way. C does not
exclude "notably some siblings" — it excludes **every household containing a
sibling or stepsibling relationship**, terminating or not.

Consequences Dev should see before choosing C, stated as facts rather than as an
argument against it: §152(c)(2)(B) is a statutory branch that would then never
appear in a `Valid` input, so the `s152_c_2_B` arm cannot reach the Checkpoint 2
"every oracle arm ≥ 20 hits" gate; and any original case exercising (c)(2)(B)
drops out of the Week-1 376-case gate if the rule is in `ValidStip`. Self-edges
are not an additional cost: under V4, K has none, because `c2_A` self-edges need
`is_child_of(T,T)` or a descendant cycle and `c2_B` requires `X \== Y`.

## Rationale

The menu's core claim — that a single macro-decrement at the c1E crossing cuts
every cycle in the group — turns out to be checkable from the source rather than
assumable, and it checks out: §152 and §7703 form a closed group with exactly one
edge back into `s7703` from inside the recursive subtree. That is worth stating
plainly because it means the three routes differ only in *which households they
exclude* and *how much proof each costs*, not in whether the counter is in the
right place. Where the menu is weakest is in the accounting around that site —
three unlisted entry points and an ambiguity between a depth counter and a shared
budget — and those are cheap to fix now and expensive to discover during Week 1.

I am not ranking the routes, and the reason is not procedural. Two proposed
remedies have already been refuted by witnesses nobody anticipated, so the
honest position is that each route's soundness argument is checkable while its
*cost* is not yet known: A's and C's corpus impact is whatever the pinned audit
says, and B's is a proof burden nobody has sized. Ranking them before those two
numbers exist would repeat the mistake that produced V10.
