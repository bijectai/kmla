### 2. What replaces the completeness framing

The deeper error behind all three was asking for the wrong kind of completeness.
H4.3 does not require the serialized Household to represent a clause's ground
relation. It requires exactly two things: (a) re-grounding the serialized
Household yields the same ordered fact and stipulation lists, and (b) every
queried goal of that case (H6) has the same canonical solution set on the
original and on the serialized file.

That is an **observational** criterion, relative to what the case's own queries
can reach — not global equivalence over all bound Prolog terms. The distinction
matters in both directions:

- It makes the problem tractable. `payment_2099_999` is invisible to (b) unless
  some queried goal binds it, and no query in the case does. An infinite ground
  relation is not an obstacle to a finite Household; it is an obstacle only to
  the extensional reading I used.
- It is also bounded in scope. Grounding runs only on the 376 originals: V1 gives
  generated inputs no stipulations and no rules, so rule-bearing Households never
  arise from `gen/`. The queried goals are therefore a fixed, enumerable set, and
  (b) is checkable rather than open-ended.

**But option (A) fails on the observational reading too.** `section7703.pl:146-153`
calls `purpose_(Payment,Household)` with `Household` bound by
`patient_(Residence,Household)` — a place, which no unary event predicate
enumerates. So an event-universe domain can miss terms the statutes actually bind
into that position, which is a preservation failure under (b) and not merely
under an extensional ideal. The domain question stays open on both readings, and
what the diagnostic plus this call site jointly narrow is the field: an
active-domain option or a per-clause mode-directed one. I am not selecting
between them, and I have not measured which the corpus needs.

### 4. What still stands

The established failure and the escalation are unaffected by these corrections,

## Rationale

The pattern in my error is worth naming, because it is the same one I have
flagged in others' work twice: a number and a completeness claim derived from the
first piece of the source I read, presented without the measurement that would
have refuted them. The 2016 family was nine lines below the 2015 family, and the
"outside pair" test is a single query. Both were cheap; neither was run before the
claim went into a PROPOSED entry that Dev is meant to act on.

The substantive repair is the preservation-domain question. An amendment that
promises extensional completeness cannot be satisfied for this clause at all,
which would have pushed Dev toward either an impossible obligation or an
arbitrary domain with no stated promise. H4.3 already fixes a weaker and
achievable criterion, and stating which criterion an amendment promises is what
makes "complete" a checkable word rather than a rhetorical one.
