# A-003

## Answer

Both passages are overstatements in my A-001 restatement. Neither is a proposed
contract restriction, and neither should be implemented. The approved B003/B004
text in `docs/PROTOCOL.md` governs. A-001 is immutable and stays as written;
A-003 supersedes exactly the wording named below and nothing else.

### 1. "They never share inputs" — superseded

Superseded A-001 wording: the sentence "They never share inputs." in the **Two
corpora, two purposes** paragraph, and the word "regenerated" in "never copied,
merged, symlinked, or regenerated into `gen/out/`".

The approved rule is about **provenance and process, not about realized
values**. What B003 actually requires:

- Admission witness *files* live in `mutate/witnesses/` and are never merged or
  inserted into `gen/out/`.
- The admission search uses a seed disjoint from `gen/`, and budgets and seeds
  are recorded.
- Corpus repair is mode-level: at Checkpoint 3a Dev names a missing *input
  mode*, `gen/` is extended for that mode, and the result is a separately frozen
  v2 re-run against the same admitted mutant set.

Nothing in B003 requires the realized input values of `gen/out/` to be disjoint
from the witness values. Value coincidence is expected and is the intended
outcome of a successful repair: if Dev names the mode a survivor exploited, the
v2 corpus will very likely contain inputs equal or close in value to that
survivor's witness, and that survivor should then be killed. A
value-disjointness rule would forbid fixing the blind spot it is meant to
measure. `gen/` independence is preserved by the seed and by generating from an
approved mode specification, not by excluding values.

The circularity B003 guards against is narrower than I stated: the grading
corpus must not be built by reading a specific mutant's witness and adding that
input to kill that mutant. The approved safeguard for this is the Checkpoint 3a
routing — a generalized mode named and signed by Dev, not a witness — plus the
separate freeze and hash of v2 and the fixed admitted set. Per-mutant targeting
of corpus generation remains out of bounds; that follows from B003's
"human names missing input modes" and from the standing rule against tuning
`gen/` or the grader to raise the kill rate, not from any value-level rule.

### 2. "never into the invariant-refutation corpus" — superseded

Superseded A-001 wording: the clause "and never into the invariant-refutation
corpus" in the **Witness storage** paragraph.

There is no approved artifact called an "invariant-refutation corpus"; that
phrase was mine, and the restriction attached to it has no basis in B004. B004
explicitly permits candidate counterexamples from the frozen corpus **plus**
witness search over the statement's quantified variables, and imposes only:

- candidates do not mutate the frozen corpus;
- `REFUTED` requires a kernel-checked theorem such as `¬ Stmt model_impl`,
  proved by exhibiting concrete values and discharging by `decide` or
  kernel-checked evaluation;
- failed proof search is `PARTIAL`, never `REFUTED`;
- a checked violation takes precedence over unproved goals;
- signed statements are never weakened; proofs stay within the gate and the
  axiom whitelist.

Provenance does not invalidate a counterexample, because the kernel check *is*
the evidence. A concrete `h`, `y` that discharges `¬ Stmt impl` by `decide` is
equally valid whether it came from `gen/out/`, from a fresh witness search over
the statement's quantified variables, or from `mutate/witnesses/`. So an
admission witness may legitimately seed an invariant-refutation search.

The one thing that does not follow, and which A-001 stated correctly and which
stands: a refutation established that way is not a **differential kill**.
Differential kill is defined on the frozen corpus only. Reporting guidance from
A-001 — report invariant-refuted-but-differentially-surviving mutants in a
separate column — remains guidance, not contract.

## Rationale

The distinction that matters is where the epistemic weight sits. For kill rate,
the claim is statistical and about a corpus, so it is only meaningful if the
corpus was not constructed against the specific mutant being scored — hence a
provenance rule on how `gen/out/` is built. For invariant `REFUTED`, the claim
is a single kernel-checked theorem, so it carries its own justification and no
provenance rule is needed. I collapsed the two into one blanket "witnesses never
touch anything else," which is tighter than approved in the first case and
baseless in the second. Astra's consistency review is correct on both.

Nothing appended to `docs/DECISION_LOG.md`, which this smoke test's authorized
path list excludes in any case. Astra should treat the approved B003/B004 text
as the operative spec and treat the superseded A-001 clauses as withdrawn.
