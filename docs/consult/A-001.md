# A-001

Receipt label carried from Q-001: `orchid-4821-cobalt`.

## Answer

B003 stands as resolved. Restated independently:

**Two corpora, two purposes.** Admission decides whether a mutant is a legitimate
test subject. Grading decides whether the frozen black-box corpus catches it.
They never share inputs.

**Admission evidence (`mutate/admit.py`).** A whole-section mutant is admitted
only when all three hold:
1. it compiles on the pinned toolchain;
2. it passes `gate/check.py` unchanged (no relaxation for mutants);
3. a targeted white-box witness search finds at least one concrete input on
   which mutant output ≠ oracle output.

The search is deliberately privileged: it may read the mutation site, perturb
exactly the fields the mutated clause reads, sweep around the mutated literal,
and run large-budget random fuzzing under a seed disjoint from `gen/`. Budgets
and seeds are recorded so admission is reproducible. Admission proves
non-equivalence; it is not evidence about the grader.

**Witness storage.** Witnesses go to `mutate/witnesses/`, keyed to the mutant.
They are never copied, merged, symlinked, or regenerated into `gen/out/`, and
never into the invariant-refutation corpus. Contaminating the grading corpus
with admission witnesses would convert kill rate into a tautology.

**UNRESOLVED.** No witness within budget ⇒ `UNRESOLVED`. Such a mutant is
excluded from both numerator and denominator of the kill rate, reported as its
own per-section count, and never labelled equivalent — the search is incomplete,
not a decision procedure. A rising UNRESOLVED count is itself a reportable
finding about the operators or the budget.

**Frozen grading corpus and kill.** `gen/out/` is frozen at Checkpoint 2 and
hashed; the hash is recorded and cited in results. A **kill** is: the
differential step of the grader refutes an admitted mutant on that frozen
corpus, i.e. produces a witness drawn from `gen/out/`. Admission evidence is
not a kill. Kill rate per section = killed / admitted, with UNRESOLVED excluded
from admitted.

**Survivors.** An admitted mutant not refuted by the frozen corpus is a
survivor. By construction it has a known witness the corpus lacks, so it is a
demonstrated corpus blind spot, not an equivalent mutant and not a grader
defect. At Checkpoint 3a Dev names the missing input mode; Astra does not infer
it and does not patch `gen/` to chase a specific survivor.

**v2 reporting.** Adding input modes produces a new corpus version, frozen and
hashed separately as v2. v1 is preserved, not overwritten. The **same admitted
mutant set** is re-run against v2 — no re-admission, no added or dropped
mutants, no re-derived UNRESOLVED set — and both v1 and v2 kill rates are
reported side by side per section. Holding the denominator fixed is the whole
point: the delta then measures corpus coverage and nothing else. Mutants
admitted later, if any, are reported as a separate cohort with their own rate.

## Rationale

Kill rate is the benchmark's only accuracy claim for the grader, standing in for
the LLM judge and human raters that KMLA refuses. It is only a claim about the
grader if the mutants were shown non-equivalent by evidence the grader did not
have. Privileged white-box admission plus strictly black-box frozen-corpus
grading is what keeps those two roles apart; the frozen hash and the fixed
admitted set are what keep the number reproducible and the v1→v2 comparison
interpretable.
