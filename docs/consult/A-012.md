### 1. Installing A's predicates (part 1)

The shape is right: fact-decidable content as real `Household` predicates,
oracle-dependent content as a required `OracleGuards` field with no default, and
the obligation recorded rather than stubbed. Six review points.

**1.1 Split A's two halves rather than putting both on the class.** Uniqueness —
at most one distinct birth event per person, at most one distinct start day per
such event — is computable from `h.facts` alone and belongs beside `v1`…`v6` as a
real function today. Only the K ∧ c3 decrease condition needs `s152_c_2` and
`s152_c_3` and therefore the class. If both halves go on the class, the half that
`gen/` must honour under Dev's second condition has no executable check in
`Interface/` until the oracle lane lands, which is the one part of A that
`gen/` needs immediately. The conjunction must of course mean exactly what the
signed text means wherever each half lives.

**1.2 The class field's shape is where the year gap will silently reappear.**
`OracleGuards.noHohCycle : Household → Year → Bool` is conjoined in `Valid` at
the single year `y`. If the new field copies that shape and is conjoined only at
`y`, the R5 guard holds at the queried taxable year and says nothing about the
year the recursion is actually entered at — which for R8 is derived, not the
taxable year. That is precisely Dev's condition 1, and copying the V8 shape is
the most likely way to fail it without noticing. Either the field is year-free
and internally quantified over `r5Years`, or it is year-indexed and `Valid`
conjoins it across all 201 years. Q proposes the second; both work, and the
second makes the quantification visible at the `Valid` site.

**1.3 Quantifying over all 201 years is a strictly larger exclusion than A, and
it is a second stated limitation.** A as accepted is stated at a fixed `y`. A
household that satisfies the decrease condition at 2018 but violates it at 1975
is currently graded and would not be after this installation. Dev asked for the
same-birthday exclusion to be recorded as a stated limitation; this is a separate
one and needs its own line, or it becomes an accidental semantic decision made by
an implementation choice. It is the one such decision I found in part 1.

**1.4 A semantically neutral way to pay for it.** A's consequent — `dob(T) <
dob(D)`, or `D` birthless and a strict descendant of `T` — is year-independent.
Only the antecedent `s152_c_3(D,T,y)` is year-indexed. So "for all `y ∈ r5Years`,
every eligible pair satisfies (i) or (ii)" is *equivalent* to "for every pair
`(T,D)` such that `s152_c_3(D,T,y)` holds for **some** `y ∈ r5Years`, (i) or
(ii)". The second form short-circuits on the first witness year instead of
re-deriving the consequent 201 times, and it narrows nothing further. This
matters because `Valid` is discharged by `decide` in invariant proofs under X1,
and a 201-fold conjunction over a pair enumeration with date arithmetic is a real
kernel-cost hazard. Worth confirming the equivalence against the installed text
before relying on it.

**1.5 The zero-exclusion evidence for the originals is year-specific.**
`full-20260922-01` reports no uniqueness violations, equal-DOB pairs, self-edges
or A-condition failures among the 376 — measured at the cases' own years. Once
the conjunct is quantified over 201 years and installed in `ValidStip`, "zero
original-case exclusions" is a different claim and needs the audit re-run over
the quantified range before it is repeated. This is the concrete protection most
likely to be skipped, because the existing result looks like it already covers
it.

**1.6 `r5Years` needs one definition site, and the no-default discipline has a
limit worth stating.** If `QueryTime` re-derives `[1900 … 2100]` independently of
the list V10 checks, the carried proof is about a different list and the coupling
is cosmetic; define it once, next to `dayLo`/`dayHi`, with the V3 link in the
comment so the two cannot drift. On the class: the existing "deliberately not
given a default instance" comment is good and should extend to the new field, but
it prevents only an *absent* instance, not a trivially-true one. Nothing
mechanical distinguishes a real production guard from an always-true one, so the
protection is the recorded obligation in STATE/HANDOFF plus review, and test-only
mocks must live where a production build cannot pick them up. Say that plainly
rather than letting the class design imply more than it delivers.

### 2. `Interface/QueryTime.lean` (part 2)

**2.1 The Workday conversion is faithful to the source, not a new decision.**
`section3306.pl:716-719` reads

```prolog
s3306_c_10_A_ii(Spouse,Student,Workday) :-
    split_string(Workday,"-","",[YS,_,_]),
    atom_number(YS,Year),
    s7703(Student,Spouse,Marriage,Year),
```

so the year passed to `s7703` is the civil year of the Workday's own `YYYY`
prefix, never the enclosing taxable year. Converting to the actual civil year is
what the source does. Under V3 and D1 a fact-derived Workday is a zero-padded
four-digit year in 1900 … 2100, so the derived year lands in `r5Years` — which is
the substance of the coverage argument, and it holds only for fact-derived days.

**2.2 The missing protection is the failure branch, not the success branch.**
Q proposes that the root-year and Workday constructors fail out of range. Nothing
states what the R5 wrapper does when a constructor fails. If it yields `[]` or
`false`, that is an invented reference value of exactly the kind A-009 ruled out:
the source at that point either fails (`split_string`/`atom_number` on a
non-day term) or raises (unbound `Workday`), and neither is an empty answer. The
wrapper needs one of two dispositions, chosen deliberately: the failure is
unreachable under `Valid` and carries that proof obligation, or it is a distinct
reported outcome. A silently total fallback would make the type a liability,
because it would look like coverage.

**2.3 The type buys signature discipline, and only if there is no escape
hatch.** Q already concedes the type does not prove every caller uses it. What it
can guarantee is that no R5 wrapper *can* be called with an unchecked year —
provided the checked type exposes no total constructor from `Year`/`Int`, no
`mk!`, and no `getD`-style convenience. One such helper voids the whole
discipline, and it is the sort of thing added later for a test. Worth a comment
at the definition site saying so.

**2.4 Stipulation-supplied years are genuinely uncovered today.**
`Stip.wellFormed` is `s.args.length == s.pred.arity` (`Interface/Household.lean:324`)
— an arity check and nothing else — and `v3` constrains the year index and
`f.days` for *facts* only. So under `ValidStip`, a stipulated clause may carry a
year argument outside 1900 … 2100, enter the R5 group there, and sit outside
`r5Years` no matter how the conjunct is quantified. Since Dev directed
installation in `ValidStip` as well, this is the concrete hole in the coverage
boundary: either `Stip.wellFormed` gains a bound on year-typed arguments, or the
originals' stipulated years must be shown in range by the audit and that shown
fact recorded. The draft's zero-exclusion result does not currently answer it.

**2.5 The unbound-year path is an error path, not a divergence path.** A free
year reaching `s7703` does not enumerate: `s7703_a_1` calls
`last_day_year(Taxy,_)` → `atom_string` on an unbound term, which raises; and a
free `Workday` raises in `split_string` before any year exists. So this belongs
in G6 reference-undefined, alongside V5's `Dob_d + 7671`, rather than in the
termination story. The practical consequence for `QueryTime` is that "no checked
year can be constructed" must map to the source's *error*, and the classification
should be written down when the conjunct is installed, because a reader will
otherwise assume the checked type covers it.

**2.6 Completeness stays a prerequisite, and its shape is enumerable.** The
end-to-end claim needs every way a year can become bound in the group to land in
`r5Years`: the taxable-year argument of a top-level query in each declared mode;
`split_string` on a fact-derived day, bounded by V3; stipulation-supplied years,
per 2.4; and R8's derived year, per 2.1. That list is short enough to discharge
by inspection once the oracle signatures exist — but it cannot be discharged
before they exist, and Q is right to keep it explicit.

### 3. The generator output-domain contract (part 3)

**3.1 "Require real `Valid`" and "never inspect `Oracle/`" read as contradictory
and should be reconciled in the text.** `Valid` now depends on `OracleGuards`,
which the oracle lane supplies. The intended line is the one that already governs
V7 and V8: `gen/` may *depend on* `Valid` through `Interface/` with an instance
supplied at build time, and may not *read* `Oracle/` sources. Without that
sentence the two clauses look like they cannot both be satisfied.

**3.2 Construct-versus-filter is invisible in the contract as stated.** If `gen/`
mints freely and filters by `Valid`, the prohibited configurations are excluded
but the surviving distribution shifts silently, and an arm can starve without
anyone seeing why. The contract requires the right *output* domain and says
nothing about rejection rates. If Dev wants the shift visible, one line — report
rejections per rule — does it; I am noting the gap rather than proposing the
rule.

**3.3 "Never silently drop original cases" is scoped to the wrong lane.** The 376
originals are read and serialized by the harness/reader lane, not minted by
`gen/`. Either the clause belongs in the harness contract, or it means the
coverage runs that include the originals, in which case say which. As written it
imposes an obligation on a component that never sees them.

**3.4 The two counts must reach the artifacts the gate reads.** Reporting record
and distinct-household counts separately fixes the inflation hazard only if the
separation also appears in the frozen corpus manifest and the coverage report,
since Checkpoint 2's "≥ 10k inputs" and "≥ 20 hits per arm" are read from those
and not from the generator's log.

### 4. What this installation does and does not establish

It fixes signatures and records obligations. It does not translate any oracle
clause, does not prove R5 or R8 terminate, does not certify that the production
guard is anything but well-typed, and does not sign off Checkpoint 1 — as Dev's
last sentence says. The R5-group halt is released by Dev's installation and
re-pin for *translation* work; every termination claim stays gated on the
complete non-recursive K ∧ c3 producer, the query-mode coverage list in 2.6, and
kernel adequacy, none of which exists today.

## Rationale

The three parts share one failure mode: a mechanism that looks like a guarantee
while the guarantee lives somewhere else. A required class field with no default
prevents an absent instance but not a trivial one. A checked year type prevents
an unchecked *value* but not an unchecked *call site*, and it is worth nothing if
a convenience constructor is added later. Quantifying over 201 years closes the
derived-year gap for fact-derived days and leaves stipulated years exactly where
they were. In each case the remedy is not more machinery but writing down which
part is mechanical and which part is a recorded obligation, so the next reader
does not credit the mechanism with the obligation's work.

The one place an implementation choice would quietly change the graded domain is
1.3, and it is worth Dev's explicit line rather than absorption into "closing the
coverage gap" — the domain is the thing every published verdict is relative to,
and A was accepted at a fixed year.
