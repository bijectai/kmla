### 1. Where A-009 was wrong

A-009 §5 composes `dob(p₁) ≤ dob(p₂) ≤ … ≤ dob(p₁)` around a cycle and concludes
that all members must share one birth date. That step presumes a **function**
`dob(·)` from persons to days. Nothing signed supplies one:

- **G1/H1** keep `facts : List Fact` in source order with duplicates kept, and
  the observation semantics preserve all solutions. Predicates over the fact list
  are relations, not functions.
- **H2/H3** give `birth_/1` with `agent_`/`start_`; neither says a person is the
  `agent_` of at most one `birth_` event, nor that an event has at most one
  `start_`.
- **H5 V1–V9** impose well-formedness, ranges, acyclicity of the child graph and
  the E1/E2/E4/E5 exclusions. None of them is a uniqueness rule.
- The source chooses existentially. `s152_c_3` (`section152.pl:196-216`) binds
  `Individual_is_born` and `Taxpayer_is_born` inside the clause body and may bind
  them differently in different calls, and the under-25 conjunct
  (`section152.pl:218-224`) re-selects a birth event independently of the first
  disjunct's choice.

So the correct reading is the one Q-010 states: strict inequalities on *selected
solutions* do not induce any order on *persons*. `a` at {2000-01-01, 2002-01-01}
and `b` at {2001-01-01} satisfies `is_before` in both directions with different
witnesses, no day is shared between distinct persons, V10 does not exclude the
input, and the cycle survives — which
`docs/consult/evidence/r5_multibirth_cycle_results.json` corroborates at 10⁵ and
10⁶ inferences, with the `no_sibling_control` run terminating as before. The
`age_both_directions` probe reporting both `s152_c_3(a,b,2018)` and
`s152_c_3(b,a,2018)` succeeding in one input is the direct refutation of the
argument, independent of any budget.

**A corollary worth recording, because it removes a tempting narrow fix.** A-009
identified the non-strict `is_before` (`utils.pl:10-14`) as the second defect.
That was accurate as a description of the first witness but is not the root
cause: with a strict `<`, this fixture still satisfies 2000 < 2001 < 2002 in both
directions. Strictness is neither necessary nor sufficient to break the cycle —
quite apart from P-INTENT clause 2, which forbids the oracle to correct the
source in the first place. The root cause is existential re-selection across
multiple fact solutions.

**The generalizable lesson.** Any termination argument for this project that
speaks of "the" birth date, "the" residence interval or "the" marriage of a
person is unsound by construction, because G1/H1 preserve multiplicity and all
solutions. A-009's error is an instance of that class, not a slip in arithmetic.
I recommend — as a review task for Dev, not as a rule — that R1–R9 be re-read
specifically for this pattern. R1's bound survives it (duplicate child facts add
no edges to the V4 graph, and V4 is stated on edges), and R6/R7 lean on V7/V8,
which are defined through the oracle's own predicates rather than through
per-person attributes, so I expect them to survive too; but "I expect" is exactly
the standard that just failed, and the re-read is cheap.

### 2. What still stands from A-009

Unaffected by this correction, because none of it depends on the `dob` function:

- **§1, the finding.** R5's "the recursion follows the child relation and
  terminates under V4" is false. `s152_c_2` (`section152.pl:158-160`) is a
  disjunction; the (c)(2)(B) branch (`section152.pl:178-187`) admits the
  symmetric `is_sibling_of` (`utils.pl:131-156`) and `is_stepsibling_of`
  (`utils.pl:167-187`); V4 constrains only `son_`/`daughter_`/`father_`/`mother_`
  edges. Q-010's fixture strengthens this rather than weakening it: the divergent
  region is strictly larger than A-009 supposed.
- **§2.** No signed condition rejects either witness. The V1–V9 walk applies to
  the new fixture unchanged, and multiple `birth_` events per person violate
  nothing in V1.
- **§3.** The three evidence levels, applied identically here: inference-limit
  exhaustion is bounded-budget evidence, not a proof of divergence; the
  structural recurrence of an identical goal in a pure SLD derivation is a sound
  informal argument; "the fixture is `Valid`" remains a specification-level
  deduction with no kernel-checked certificate, since the production V7/V8 guards
  do not exist.
- **§4.** This is R9's design-flaw case. Fuel exhaustion must not stand in as a
  reference value: the pinned program itself diverges, so there is no reference
  answer, and a Lean side returning `[]` would invent one. The remedy belongs in
  signed H5.
- **§6.** The halt scope is unchanged — §7703, §152, and every target whose call
  graph reaches `s152_a_1`/`s7703_b_1`, including R8's entry from
  `s3306_c_10_A_ii`.

### 3. The proof obligation left open

I am not substituting another rule. For any candidate remedy — Dev's or a later
proposal — these are the obligations that must be discharged, and the one A-009
skipped is (a):

- **(a) Define the step relation over all permitted fact solutions.** The
  recursion's state is a call, not a person: `Taxp ⟶ Dep` holds iff *some*
  solution of §152(c)(1)(A)∧(B)∧(C) with those arguments succeeds. The relation
  is the union over all witness choices. Any argument that fixes one witness per
  person bounds a sub-relation and says nothing about the rest.
- **(b) Exhibit a well-founded measure that strictly decreases along that
  relation**, or show the relation is acyclic outright. It must be a measure on
  the call state, computed from the whole Household, not from a chosen witness.
- **(c) Show `Valid` decides the measure's premises.** Decidable from the fact
  list, as V1–V6 are, or with an explicit `Oracle/` import, as V7 and V8 are. The
  cost of the over-approximation should be stated, in V7's style.
- **(d) Derive the fuel constant from the measure**, rather than carrying over
  R5's `persons.length + 1`, whose justification is the one that failed. Cover
  R8, which inherits it.
- **(e) Check the candidate against both preserved witnesses** —
  `r5_sibling_cycle.pl` and `r5_multibirth_cycle.pl` — before it is signed. Each
  refuted a predecessor; a third witness is likelier than not, and finding it
  before Dev signs is much cheaper than after.
- **(f) If the candidate adds a uniqueness requirement** (one `birth_` per
  person, or one `start_` per event), recognise that as narrowing the signed
  domain, not as a technical repair. It removes a class of adversarial inputs the
  benchmark may want and would need V1 amended. That is Dev's judgment call, and
  it is the kind of decision that must be made deliberately rather than as a side
  effect of making a termination proof go through.

## Rationale

Withdrawing cleanly matters more here than salvaging the proposal. A design
authority's output in this lane is a claim Dev will sign into `human/DECISIONS.md`
and will not re-derive, and the failure mode of a plausible-but-unsound
termination argument is silent: the fuel bound looks fine, the oracle compiles,
and the divergence shows up as a timeout that the harness classifies as an
infrastructure failure rather than as a mismatch. That is precisely the class of
error the parity meter cannot catch, which is why R9 pre-commits to treating it
as a design flaw.

The reason I am not offering V11 is not diffidence. It is that the obvious next
candidates — uniqueness of birth facts, or a measure over (Dependent, Taxpayer)
pairs — either narrow the signed domain in ways only Dev should weigh, or need
the same kind of argument that has now failed twice on witnesses produced within
a day. The useful thing to hand Dev is the obligation list and the two witnesses,
so the next proposal is tested before it is signed rather than after.
